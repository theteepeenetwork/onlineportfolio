Storyjar icon set

One family of hand-inked icons children learn once and read everywhere — tools, adding a moment, the jar, and getting around.

The standard

Every icon is drawn on one 24-unit keyline grid so weight, corners and optical size match — a child who learns the pen recognises the whole set. One shape, one accent colour, ink everywhere else.

24-unit keyline

All artwork lives in a 0 0 24 24 box. Shapes snap to the grid so nothing looks heavier than its neighbour.

2px ink stroke

Outlines are #22304A, 2px, with round caps & joins — the same friendly hand as the jar.

One accent

A single fill from the tile palette per icon. Never two accents fighting; the rest stays ink-on-paper.

Two sizes

Child 44px inside a ≥64px target; teacher 24px. Same file, no redraw.

Kit glyphs

One glyph per kit in the ＋ fan, sibling to Shapes. The hard constraint: a kit glyph must not read as another cluster of shapes, or a child taps Shapes expecting the kit. Five candidates for Maths — pick one and I'll drop the rest.

Each shown at fan size (44px) and at the 64px touch floor.

A · Base-10 trio Flat, rod, one. Says "apparatus" instantly to anyone who teaches KS1. Busiest of the five at 44px.
B · Ten frame Three counters in a 5×2. Reads as counting, not shapes. Wide box wastes the square fan button.
C · Number line + hop Survives 44px best of the five, and the hop reads as doing maths rather than owning maths objects.
D · Abacus Warmest and most physical. Caveat: the kit contains no abacus, so it promises a thing that isn't there.
E · Mixed apparatus Rod, half-circle, counter. Covers the kit's breadth, and is the one that most risks reading as "shapes".

Preset geometry — the spec, not the art

These are not icons. Each one is what shapeParts must output, drawn on a 100-unit model box at the same ratio the renderers use: outline stroked at 4, detail at 4 × 0.4 = 1.6, floored at 1 model unit. The palette button and the canvas object come from this one function, so they cannot drift.

Each card shows the preset at button size (64px) and at half size (32px), which is the legibility test the detail ratio exists to pass.
